Comprehensive Course Design

Braking Barriers: Empowering Women in Car Buying

An online course helping women build the car knowledge and confidence to buy a car independently — designed as part of Carnegie Mellon's METALS program.

?

Challenge

Many women lack confidence in car-buying due to limited knowledge, leading to hesitation and reliance on others: a knowledge gap rooted in cultural norms (cars framed as a "man's hobby") and a lack of hands-on exposure growing up.

→
→

Approach

A community-oriented, self-paced online course covering fundamental vehicle mechanics, car evaluation, and purchasing strategies, built around interactive multimedia, guided discovery, and scaffolded, collaborative learning. Understanding car mechanics and purchasing processes fosters independence, reduces vulnerability to misinformation, and empowers confident decision-making.

Big ideas

1

Self-paced, asynchronous online class

2

Women's empowerment in a male-dominated topic

3

Practical, decision-making skills

4

Interactive and multimedia learning

Essential questions

1

What are the components of a car, and their functions?

2

How do we maintain a car effectively?

3

How do we choose the right car for our needs?

Learning outcomes

1

Identify different car types and their functionalities

2

Evaluate car features and mechanics

3

Navigate the car-buying process independently

Instructional design framework

The course runs as a self-paced online course hosted on Her Campus, accessible on mobile and desktop, built for young women in Pennsylvania with little car-buying experience, using a community-oriented approach to create a supportive, inclusive learning environment.

The pedagogical approach combines:

1

Interactive multimedia

Videos, infographics, and clickable diagrams

2

Guided discovery

Hands-on activities like Car Evaluation and Selection

3

Collaborative learning

Discussion boards and peer feedback

4

Scaffolded learning

Foundational knowledge building toward practical application

Course structure

1

Unit 1

Car Types, Features, and Suitability

2

Unit 2

Car Components and Maintenance Basics

3

Unit 3

Evaluating Cars Based on Budget, Safety, Reliability

4

Unit 4

Common Car Issues & How to Handle Them

5

Unit 5

The Car Buying Process (specific to Pennsylvania)

Assessment design

Each unit pairs a pre-test and post-test with a discussion board reflection, so learners get immediate corrective feedback early and delayed, explanatory feedback as they go deeper. Assessments take three forms:

1

Quizzes

Knowledge checks at different stages

2

Discussion boards

Learners reflect on scenarios and engage with peers

3

Self-assessment

Reflective practice on confidence and application

Example quiz questions:

1

Matching question

Identify car components in a labeled diagram

2

Scenario-based question

"Your car won't start, and you hear a clicking sound. What's likely the issue?" (a) Out of fuel (b) Dead battery (correct answer: b)

3

Decision-making question

Help a friend select a car based on budget, safety, and lifestyle

Rubrics for evaluation

1

Discussion board participation

Scored based on engagement, clarity, and relevance

2

Self-assessment criteria

Learners reflect on knowledge gained and future application

Interactive activities

1

Car Evaluation & Selection

  1. Learn about car safety, reliability, and test-drive considerations through interactive videos
  2. Compare different car options using an interactive comparison tool
  3. Select the most suitable car and justify the decision in a written or audio format
2

Clickable Car Component Diagram

Click on parts of a car image to reveal descriptions and functions

Course implementation & evaluation

1

Delivery format

Asynchronous, self-paced learning, advertised through DMVs, driving schools, and colleges

2

Evaluation metrics

Pre/post assessments to measure improvement, engagement metrics (discussion board participation, quiz attempts), and learner feedback surveys for content effectiveness

What's next

Planned expansions include additional modules on financing and negotiating car deals, and extending the course beyond Pennsylvania to other states.
